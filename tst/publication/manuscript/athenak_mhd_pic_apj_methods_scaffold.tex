\begin{abstract}
Astrophysical systems containing energetic particles span scales that are
difficult to resolve with fully kinetic methods. MHD-PIC addresses this scale
separation by evolving the thermal plasma as magnetohydrodynamic gas while
advancing a selected energetic-particle population kinetically
\citep{BaiEtAl2015,SunBai2023,MignoneEtAl2018,vanMarleEtAl2018}.
We describe the physical model, discrete coupling, mesh-interface choices, and
evidence architecture of the \athenak implementation. The active
paper-reproduction candidate stores mass-normalized particle momentum, advances
it with a relativistic Boris pusher, uses triangular-shaped-cloud
interpolation and deposition, retains ideal-MHD induction, and returns the
opposite particle momentum and kinetic-energy changes to an ideal-MHD gas on
the bounded uniform-mesh path. A source audit exposes two boundaries that
control interpretation. First, the discrete conversion from macro-particle
current contributions to deposited current density $J_{\rm CR}/c$ requires
division by recipient-cell volume; historical Bell preparation that omitted
this conversion and multiplied the closure by the artificial particle light
speed represents a different physical problem. Second, receiver-resolution
deposition at a refinement interface trades exact individual-particle
conservation for a profile designed to remain locally smooth, so uniform-mesh
conservation cannot be transferred to adaptive meshes. We consequently define
a verification hierarchy that separates operator-level checks, distributed
execution and restart tests, registered physical campaigns, and
provenance-qualified manuscript claims.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The central numerical challenge in MHD-PIC is not advancing either an MHD
state or a particle orbit in isolation. It is preserving a physically
interpretable exchange between them while particles cross cells, ranks,
boundaries, and refinement levels. An implementation can pass a local orbit
test yet represent the wrong instability if its deposited current has the
wrong normalization. It can conserve momentum on a uniform periodic mesh yet
lose that property at a refinement interface. It can also produce plausible
fields while lacking evidence that the analyzed bytes came from the registered
executable and run. This paper therefore asks under what documented physical,
numerical, and evidentiary conditions \athenak can support corrected Bell
calculations and a Section~5.4 parallel shock.

MHD-PIC provides the required scale reduction by retaining kinetic energetic
particles while replacing the thermal plasma with an MHD fluid
\citep{BaiEtAl2015,MignoneEtAl2018,vanMarleEtAl2018,SunBai2023}. The reduction
is useful precisely because it is selective: particle gyromotion,
distribution evolution, and particle--gas exchange remain explicit, whereas
thermal kinetic scales and thermal-pool injection do not. A methods claim must
therefore state both the numerical accuracy of the retained dynamics and the
physical questions excluded by the approximation.

Two campaign-level problems organize the validation. The Bell current-driven
instability is sensitive to the magnitude and sign of the energetic-particle
current, making it a direct test of current deposition and backreaction
\citep{Bell2004}. Its nonlinear development has been studied with fully
kinetic and hybrid approaches \citep{RiquelmeSpitkovsky2009,GargateEtAl2010,
ZacharegkasEtAl2024}; those studies provide comparison context only when
dimensionless regimes and observables are matched. The second target is the
non-relativistic parallel shock in Section~5.4 of \citet{SunBai2023},
interpreted alongside the MHD-PIC shock setup of \citet{BaiEtAl2015}. It
combines injection, open and reflecting boundaries, particle provenance,
adaptive refinement, restart, and long-time acceleration in one calculation.

Our methodological contribution is an auditable composition of the physical
model, discrete update, and claim-evidence chain. We first identify the exact
runtime identity and approximation envelope. We then derive the implemented
particle and gas exchange, explain the current-normalization and
refinement-interface choices that determine the represented physics, and
separate local verification from distributed and campaign-level
qualification. This organization is deliberate: a result is interpretable
only after the method and the evidence path that produced it are both fixed.

\section{Physical Model and Notation}
\label{sec:model}

This section must first state the continuum model, then identify the discrete
choices that materially affect interpretation.

\subsection{Energetic-Particle State and Equation of Motion}

The paper-mode particle state stores mass-normalized momentum
$\boldsymbol{w}\equiv\boldsymbol{p}/m$. The derived Lorentz factor, velocity,
and kinetic energy per unit mass are
\begin{align}
\gamma &=
\left(1+\frac{|\boldsymbol{w}|^2}{\mathbb{C}^2}\right)^{1/2},
\label{eq:gamma}\\
\boldsymbol{v} &= \frac{\boldsymbol{w}}{\gamma},
\label{eq:velocity}\\
\epsilon_{\rm kin} &= (\gamma-1)\mathbb{C}^2.
\label{eq:kinetic-energy}
\end{align}
Here $\mathbb{C}$ is AthenaK's configured artificial particle light speed,
following the MHD-PIC modeling choice used by \citet{BaiEtAl2015} and
\citet{SunBai2023}.
All explicit paper and extension identities interpret the retained particle
state slots as $\boldsymbol{w}$; physical velocity is derived from
Equation~\ref{eq:velocity} for drift, deposition, VTK and tracked-particle
output, and timestep selection. Initialization may be specified as velocity, in
which case the initializer converts it to mass-normalized momentum. The parser
requires every velocity-initialized species to satisfy
$|\boldsymbol{v}|<\mathbb{C}$. This artificial particle-light-speed choice is
not self-validating: the represented particle, gas, Alfv\'en, and signal speeds
must remain well below $\mathbb{C}$, and every physical campaign must
demonstrate that its reported observables are insensitive to a preregistered
increase in $\mathbb{C}$. The parser speed bound is necessary but is not that
physical convergence test.

The particle equations advanced in paper mode are
\begin{align}
\frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt} &= \boldsymbol{v},
\label{eq:particle-position}\\
\frac{d\boldsymbol{w}}{dt} &=
\frac{q}{mc}\left(c\boldsymbol{E}+\boldsymbol{v}\times\boldsymbol{B}\right),
\label{eq:particle-momentum}\\
c\boldsymbol{E} &= -\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{B}.
\label{eq:ideal-electric-field}
\end{align}
Equations~\ref{eq:particle-position}--\ref{eq:ideal-electric-field} follow the
MHD-PIC formulation used by \citet{BaiEtAl2015} and \citet{SunBai2023}.
AthenaK advances Equation~\ref{eq:particle-momentum} with the relativistic
Boris rotation, matching the pusher named by \citet{SunBai2023}.
\citet{BirdsallLangdon2018} provides broader particle-in-cell context and is
cited below for TSC deposition. For fields interpolated at the midpoint
particle position, define
\begin{align}
\alpha &\equiv \frac{q}{mc}\frac{\Delta t}{2}, &
\boldsymbol{w}^{-} &=
\boldsymbol{w}^{n}+\alpha c\boldsymbol{E}^{n+1/2},
\label{eq:boris-electric-minus}\\
\gamma^{-} &=
\left(1+\frac{|\boldsymbol{w}^{-}|^2}{\mathbb{C}^2}\right)^{1/2}, &
\boldsymbol{t} &= \frac{\alpha\boldsymbol{B}^{n+1/2}}{\gamma^{-}},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{s}=\frac{2\boldsymbol{t}}{1+|\boldsymbol{t}|^2},
\label{eq:boris-rotation-vectors}\\
\boldsymbol{w}' &= \boldsymbol{w}^{-}
+ \boldsymbol{w}^{-}\times\boldsymbol{t}, &
\boldsymbol{w}^{+} &= \boldsymbol{w}^{-}
+ \boldsymbol{w}'\times\boldsymbol{s},
\label{eq:boris-rotation}\\
\boldsymbol{w}^{n+1} &= \boldsymbol{w}^{+}
+\alpha c\boldsymbol{E}^{n+1/2}.
\label{eq:boris-electric-plus}
\end{align}
These equations reproduce the implemented operation order: half electric
acceleration, magnetic rotation using $\gamma^{-}$, and the second half
electric acceleration. The magnetic substep preserves
$|\boldsymbol{w}^{-}|$ algebraically, whereas changes in particle kinetic
energy arise through the electric substeps. The particle timestep is restricted
by both a configured maximum cell crossing and a configured maximum Boris gyro
angle. These are numerical guards; they do not by themselves establish that a
selected artificial light speed is physically adequate.

\subsection{Deposited-Current Normalization}

The cell-centered current moment deposited by the general particle path is
\begin{equation}
\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{J}_{\rm CR}}{c}\right)_i
=
\frac{1}{V_i}\sum_p
\eta_p q_{\rm scale} W_p f_p q_{s(p)}
\boldsymbol{v}_p S_{ip},
\label{eq:generic-deposited-current}
\end{equation}
where $S_{ip}$ is the selected particle-shape contribution to recipient cell
$i$, $\eta_p$ is the physical-density scale, and $f_p$ is the evolving
delta-$f$ weight or unity for a full-$f$ particle. Thus particle statistical
weight, delta-$f$ weighting, physical-density scaling, particle shape, and
recipient-cell volume are part of the generic deposition contract.

For the non-expanding, full-$f$, uniform Q043 Bell carrier, $\eta_p=f_p=W_p=1$
and the uniform shape sum reduces Equation~\ref{eq:generic-deposited-current}
to
\begin{equation}
\left(\frac{J_{\rm CR}}{c}\right)_{\rm dep}
=
\frac{{\rm PPC}\,q_{\rm scale}\,q_s\,v_{\rm CR}}
     {V_{\rm root\,cell}}
=2B_gk_0,
\label{eq:bell-deposited-current}
\end{equation}
where ${\rm PPC}$ is particles per root cell, $q_{\rm scale}$ is
\texttt{deposit\_qscale}, $q_s$ is the configured species charge, and
$V_{\rm root\,cell}$ is computed from the global root-mesh extents and counts.
The configured charge and charge-to-mass factor must remain distinct:
$q_s=m_s[q/(mc)]$, with the shorthand $q_s=q/(mc)$ valid only when $m_s=1$.
The corrected source-local current-oracle matrix uses $m_s=2$ and separately
binds $q_s/m_s$ to the particle equation of motion, enforcing this distinction;
its complete registered runtime observation remains pending. A fixed
${\rm PPC}\,q_{\rm scale}$ therefore changes the physical current when
dimension or root resolution changes unless the volume dependence is included.
Changing only meshblock/rank decomposition must leave
Equation~\ref{eq:bell-deposited-current} invariant.

The historical Bell preparation omitted $V_{\rm root\,cell}$ from its closure
and targeted $2B_g\mathbb{C}k_0$ rather than $2B_gk_0$. Both errors change the
physical Bell problem and invalidate every inherited historical Bell artifact
for qualification.

\subsection{MHD Equations and Particle Backreaction}

The background plasma follows non-relativistic ideal MHD. In \runtime, the
induction update remains the ordinary constrained-transport update formed from
Equation~\ref{eq:ideal-electric-field}; the parser rejects direct
CR-current-to-CT options and requires the CR Hall mode to be off. Particle
backreaction therefore enters the gas momentum and, for an ideal equation of
state, total-energy updates, but not the paper-mode induction electric field.
In AthenaK's $4\pi=1$ magnetic normalization, the continuum equations represented
by that path are
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}
 + \boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot(\rho\boldsymbol{u}) &= 0,
\label{eq:mhd-mass}\\
\frac{\partial(\rho\boldsymbol{u})}{\partial t}
 + \boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\left[
\rho\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{u}
+\left(P+\frac{B^2}{2}\right)\boldsymbol{I}
-\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}\right] &= \boldsymbol{S}_{P,g},
\label{eq:mhd-momentum}\\
\frac{\partial E_g}{\partial t}
 + \boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\left[
\left(E_g+P+\frac{B^2}{2}\right)\boldsymbol{u}
-\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{B})\right] &= S_{E,g},
\label{eq:mhd-energy}\\
\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{B}}{\partial t}
-\boldsymbol{\nabla}\times(\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{B}) &= 0,
\qquad \boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\boldsymbol{B}=0,
\label{eq:mhd-induction}\\
E_g &= \frac{P}{\gamma_g-1}
+\frac{\rho u^2}{2}+\frac{B^2}{2}.
\label{eq:mhd-total-energy}
\end{align}
The ideal-gas path uses $P=(\gamma_g-1)e_{\rm int}$. Exact-isothermal
compositions omit Equation~\ref{eq:mhd-energy} and close with
$P=c_s^2\rho$. In the full-$f$ opposite-impulse path,
$\boldsymbol{S}_{P,g}$ and $S_{E,g}$ are the negatives of the deposited
particle momentum and kinetic-energy rates defined below.

For a full-$f$ particle $p$ of species $s$, macro-particle weight $W_p$, and
macro mass
\begin{equation}
M_p=q_{\rm scale}W_p m_s,
\label{eq:macro-mass}
\end{equation}
the full-step midpoint Boris kick records the extensive rates
\begin{align}
\dot{\boldsymbol{P}}_p &=
\frac{M_p}{\Delta t}
\left(\boldsymbol{w}^{n+1}_p-\boldsymbol{w}^{n}_p\right),
\label{eq:particle-momentum-rate}\\
\dot{E}_p &=
\frac{M_p}{\Delta t}
\left[\epsilon_{\rm kin}(\boldsymbol{w}^{n+1}_p)
      -\epsilon_{\rm kin}(\boldsymbol{w}^{n}_p)\right].
\label{eq:particle-energy-rate}
\end{align}
Let ${\cal D}_i$ denote deposition to cell $i$, including the selected shape
weights and division by recipient-cell volume. At the impulse-feedback stage,
the gas update applies the opposite deposited change,
\begin{align}
(\rho\boldsymbol{u})_i &\leftarrow
(\rho\boldsymbol{u})_i-\beta\Delta t\,
{\cal D}_i(\dot{\boldsymbol{P}}_p),
\label{eq:gas-opposite-momentum}\\
E_i &\leftarrow E_i-\beta\Delta t\,{\cal D}_i(\dot{E}_p),
\label{eq:gas-opposite-energy}
\end{align}
where $\beta\Delta t$ is the active MHD stage interval. The VL2 predictor uses
the initial-position deposited charge and current to form the corresponding
Lorentz-force predictor; the corrector consumes the deposited particle
impulse. Unit momentum and energy feedback coefficients are mandatory for
\runtime. Exact-isothermal paper delta-$f$, and the separately bounded Q-006
full-$f$ carrier, use momentum-only feedback because no ideal-MHD total-energy
variable is present.

On a uniform periodic mesh, with full-$f$ particles, unit feedback
coefficients, normalized shape sums, no floors, and no particle creation,
removal, or escape, summing Equations~\ref{eq:gas-opposite-momentum} and
\ref{eq:gas-opposite-energy} over cells cancels the corresponding summed
particle changes. This is the exact global conservation invariant tested by
the uniform-mesh coupling path. It is not a generic conservation claim for
open or reflecting boundaries, shock injection and removal, delta-$f$
feedback, exact-isothermal energy, floors, or refinement interfaces; those
terms require separate accounting and qualification.

\section{Numerical Integration and Software Architecture}
\label{sec:numerics}

The equations in Section~\ref{sec:model} become a coupled method only after
their stage ordering, interpolation, deposition, boundary handling, and
restart semantics are specified. The important distinction in this section is
between operations that are locally second order and compositions whose
validity depends on mesh or execution topology.

\subsection{Second-Order Coupled Stage Sequence}

The intended coupled sequence is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Deposit initial-position particle moments required by the gas predictor.
\item Advance particle positions to the midpoint.
\item Advance the MHD predictor and apply documented particle source terms.
\item Interpolate midpoint gas velocity and magnetic field to particles.
\item Form Equation~\ref{eq:ideal-electric-field} and advance momentum with the
relativistic Boris update.
\item Record particle momentum and kinetic-energy changes.
\item Advance particle positions to the final state.
\item Deposit recorded changes and apply the opposite gas update.
\item Apply physical boundaries, migration, synchronization, and constrained
transport in the documented task order.
\end{enumerate}
The implementation realizes this sequence with two VL2 stages having
$\beta_1=1/2$ and $\beta_2=1$, following the VL2 integration framework of
\citet{StoneGardiner2009}. During stage 1, the particle momentum push is a
no-op: charge and current are deposited at $\boldsymbol{x}^n$, synchronized,
and used by the gas predictor through the normalized
$\rho_{\rm CR}(c\boldsymbol{E})+(\boldsymbol{J}_{\rm CR}/c)\times\boldsymbol{B}$
and $(\boldsymbol{J}_{\rm CR}/c)\cdot(c\boldsymbol{E})$ sources. Particles then
drift by $\Delta t/2$ to $\boldsymbol{x}^{n+1/2}$. During stage 2, TSC
interpolation samples the midpoint gas state, the Boris update advances
$\boldsymbol{w}$ over the full $\Delta t$, and the code deposits
$\dot{\boldsymbol{P}}_p$ and $\dot{E}_p$. The gas corrector subtracts those
deposited rates with $\beta_2=1$, after which particles drift by the remaining
$\Delta t/2$ to $\boldsymbol{x}^{n+1}$. Moment boundary exchange and
restriction/prolongation precede each gas source evaluation; particle boundary
handling and migration follow each completed stage.

deposition \citep{BirdsallLangdon2018}. In each active dimension, the quadratic
three-point shape is used to interpolate the midpoint MHD velocity and magnetic
field and to deposit charge, current, and the extensive feedback rates. Charge
and current use the macro charge $q_{\rm scale}W_pq_s$ and physical velocity;
feedback channels use Equations~\ref{eq:particle-momentum-rate} and
\ref{eq:particle-energy-rate}. Deposited extensive rates are divided by the
recipient-cell volume before entering Equations~\ref{eq:gas-opposite-momentum}
and \ref{eq:gas-opposite-energy}.

For a cell center separated from the particle by $r$ cell widths, the
implemented one-dimensional shape is
\begin{equation}
S_2(r)=
\begin{cases}
3/4-r^2, & |r|\leq 1/2,\\
\frac{1}{2}(3/2-|r|)^2, & 1/2<|r|\leq 3/2,\\
0, & |r|>3/2.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:tsc-shape}
\end{equation}
The multidimensional weight is the tensor product of
Equation~\ref{eq:tsc-shape} over active dimensions. On a uniform periodic
mesh, the three weights in each active dimension sum to unity. This
partition-of-unity property is the discrete step that allows the summed
opposite-impulse update to cancel the summed particle impulse in the
uniform-mesh conservation envelope.

On multilevel meshes, \runtime selects the separately named
\texttt{paper\_smooth} candidate path, which routes receiver-resolution TSC
records to leaf blocks. This choice follows the requirement that each
cross-interface contribution be evaluated at the receiving-cell resolution;
the weight sum associated with one particle may therefore differ from unity
near a refinement interface while the deposited profile is designed to remain
locally smooth.

\section{Discussion: Applicability, Failure Modes, and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The method is best understood as a chain of controlled approximations. MHD-PIC
removes thermal kinetic scales while retaining an explicitly kinetic energetic
population. Paper mode then narrows the electromagnetic coupling further by
using ideal-MHD induction without a CR Hall term. Finally, numerical choices
such as artificial particle light speed, particle shape, mesh-interface
policy, and injection prescription select a computable member of that physical
model. The value of the implementation follows from making this chain
auditable, not from treating any one member as general plasma physics.

The Bell current-normalization defect illustrates why source audit and physical
validation cannot be separated. Omitting the root-cell volume changes the
driving current when dimension or resolution changes; multiplying deposited
$J_{\rm CR}/c$ by $\mathbb{C}$ changes the target mode itself. A run may remain
stable and display filamentary structure under either error. Stability and
morphological plausibility therefore cannot validate the represented Bell
problem. The corrected direct-current oracle, corrected linear predecessor,
and a physics-first nonlinear sensitivity matrix are necessary in that order.

The AMR interface presents a different tradeoff. Evaluating TSC at receiver
resolution targets the intended local smoothness but sacrifices exact
per-particle partition of unity at mixed levels. Renormalizing the support can
restore extensive conservation while changing the deposited profile and
introducing additional communication and computation. Neither choice is
universally preferable; each defines a distinct numerical method whose
smoothness, conservation, noise, scaling, and nonlinear consequences must be
measured. The current paper candidate uses \texttt{paper\_smooth}, so exact
uniform-mesh conservation is not an AMR claim.

The parallel-shock calculation adds model uncertainty beyond numerical error.
Its ideal moving injection surface and monoenergetic isotropic source provide a
controlled energetic-particle population, but they do not resolve thermal-pool
injection. The detected shock front need not coincide with the ideal injection
surface, and the selected $P_0=1$ baseline is a provenance decision rather
than evidence of pressure independence. Any accepted Section~5.4 comparison
must preserve these distinctions.

The strongest remaining objection is evidentiary: bounded local or selected
accelerator checks do not establish long-horizon physical fidelity, and an
internally consistent receipt does not prove that registered bytes were the
bytes analyzed. The current Q043 hardening review demonstrates this point by
exposing path-substitution and reopen-after-verification gaps despite extensive
negative tests. We therefore treat trusted execution provenance as part of
numerical qualification rather than post-processing administration.

\begin{itemize}
\item The paper-mode formulation excludes CR Hall induction; Hall behavior is a
separate extension.
\item Corrected Bell claims require the full volume-aware deposited-current
closure and demonstrated dimension/resolution/decomposition invariance.
\item Native AthenaK particle runs require a 2D or 3D mesh. A
``one-dimensional'' Bell result means one-dimensional variation carried on a
thin 2D/3V mesh, not a native 1D particle calculation.
\item Exact opposite-impulse global conservation is claimed only for its
explicitly qualified uniform-mesh, full-$f$, closed-domain envelope.
\item MHD-PIC does not resolve thermal-pool injection physics in the Section~5.4
shock setup.
\item The selected artificial particle light speed requires campaign-specific
convergence; parser enforcement of subluminal initialized velocity is not a
physical adequacy test.
\item \texttt{paper\_smooth} AMR deposition and a possible conservative
extension are different numerical policies; neither may inherit the other's
claims.

\section{Reproducibility and Evidence Provenance}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

Reproducibility in this work means more than rerunning a command. A
result-bearing evidence chain must bind (1) the source tree and dependencies,
(2) the built executable and environment profile, (3) the exact input deck,
runtime parameters, and registered policy slice, (4) scheduler identity and
terminal state, (5) the complete raw-artifact inventory, (6) deterministic
analysis and figure/table reconstruction, and (7) the accepted claim scope.
Each link must fail closed if an expected object is absent, mutable,
substituted, or inconsistent with the preceding link.

Registered runs use one immutable run root of the form
\path{runs/<campaign>/<submission-id>/}. Reservation, scheduler attachment,
cancellation, and reconciliation events enter an append-only hash-chained
node-hour ledger. Compact ledger state is mirrored separately from the Orion
bulk-evidence root, while each run retains exact decks, launch metadata,
scheduler records, raw products, and inventories. The mirror proves accounting
continuity; it does not turn Orion-only bulk retention into a durable
institutional archive.

must independently verify the installed control-plane generation, scheduler
