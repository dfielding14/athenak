\subsection{Energetic-Particle State and Equation of Motion}

The paper-mode particle state stores mass-normalized momentum
$\boldsymbol{w}\equiv\boldsymbol{p}/m$. The derived Lorentz factor, velocity,
and kinetic energy per unit mass are
\begin{align}
\gamma &=
\left(1+\frac{|\boldsymbol{w}|^2}{\mathbb{C}^2}\right)^{1/2},
\label{eq:gamma}\\
\boldsymbol{v} &= \frac{\boldsymbol{w}}{\gamma},
\label{eq:velocity}\\
\epsilon_{\rm kin} &= (\gamma-1)\mathbb{C}^2.
\label{eq:kinetic-energy}
\end{align}
Here $\mathbb{C}$ is the configured artificial cosmic-ray light speed.
All explicit paper and extension identities interpret the retained particle
state slots as $\boldsymbol{w}$; physical velocity is derived from
Equation~\ref{eq:velocity} for drift, deposition, VTK and tracked-particle
output, and timestep selection. Initialization may be specified as velocity, in
which case the initializer converts it to mass-normalized momentum. The parser
requires every velocity-initialized species to satisfy
$|\boldsymbol{v}|<\mathbb{C}$. The reduced-speed approximation additionally
requires the represented particle, gas, Alfv\'en, and signal speeds to remain
well below $\mathbb{C}$, and every physical campaign must demonstrate that its
reported observables are insensitive to a preregistered increase in
$\mathbb{C}$. The parser speed bound is necessary but is not that physical
sensitivity test.

The particle equations advanced in paper mode are
\begin{align}
\frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt} &= \boldsymbol{v},
\label{eq:particle-position}\\
\frac{d\boldsymbol{w}}{dt} &=
\frac{q}{mc}\left(c\boldsymbol{E}+\boldsymbol{v}\times\boldsymbol{B}\right),
\label{eq:particle-momentum}\\
c\boldsymbol{E} &= -\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{B}.
\label{eq:ideal-electric-field}
\end{align}

\subsection{Deposited-Current Normalization}

The cell-centered current moment deposited by the general particle path is
\begin{equation}
\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{J}_{\rm CR}}{c}\right)_i
=
\frac{1}{V_i}\sum_p
\eta_p q_{\rm scale} W_p f_p q_{s(p)}
\boldsymbol{v}_p S_{ip},
\label{eq:generic-deposited-current}
\end{equation}
where $S_{ip}$ is the selected particle-shape contribution to recipient cell
$i$, $\eta_p$ is the physical-density scale, and $f_p$ is the evolving
delta-$f$ weight or unity for a full-$f$ particle. Thus particle statistical
weight, delta-$f$ weighting, physical-density scaling, particle shape, and
recipient-cell volume are part of the generic deposition contract.

For the non-expanding, full-$f$, uniform Q043 Bell carrier, $\eta_p=f_p=W_p=1$
and the uniform shape sum reduces Equation~\ref{eq:generic-deposited-current}
to
\begin{equation}
\left(\frac{J_{\rm CR}}{c}\right)_{\rm dep}
=
\frac{{\rm PPC}\,q_{\rm scale}\,q_s\,v_{\rm CR}}
     {V_{\rm root\,cell}}
=2B_gk_0,
\label{eq:bell-deposited-current}
\end{equation}
where ${\rm PPC}$ is particles per root cell, $q_{\rm scale}$ is
\texttt{deposit\_qscale}, $q_s$ is the configured species charge, and
$V_{\rm root\,cell}$ is computed from the global root-mesh extents and counts.
The configured charge and charge-to-mass factor must remain distinct:
$q_s=m_s[q/(mc)]$, with the shorthand $q_s=q/(mc)$ valid only when $m_s=1$.
The corrected source-local current-oracle matrix uses $m_s=2$ and separately
binds $q_s/m_s$ to the particle equation of motion, enforcing this distinction;
its complete registered runtime observation remains pending. A fixed
${\rm PPC}\,q_{\rm scale}$ therefore changes the physical current when
dimension or root resolution changes unless the volume dependence is included.
Changing only meshblock/rank decomposition must leave
Equation~\ref{eq:bell-deposited-current} invariant.

The historical Bell preparation omitted $V_{\rm root\,cell}$ from its closure
and targeted $2B_g\mathbb{C}k_0$ rather than $2B_gk_0$. Both errors change the
physical Bell problem and invalidate every inherited historical Bell artifact
for qualification.

\subsection{MHD Equations and Particle Backreaction}

The background plasma follows non-relativistic ideal MHD. In \runtime, the
induction update remains the ordinary constrained-transport update formed from
Equation~\ref{eq:ideal-electric-field}; the parser rejects direct
CR-current-to-CT options and requires the CR Hall mode to be off. Particle
backreaction therefore enters the gas momentum and, for an ideal equation of
state, total-energy updates, but not the paper-mode induction electric field.
In AthenaK's $4\pi=1$ magnetic normalization, the continuum equations represented
by that path are
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}
 + \boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot(\rho\boldsymbol{u}) &= 0,
\label{eq:mhd-mass}\\
\frac{\partial(\rho\boldsymbol{u})}{\partial t}
 + \boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\left[
\rho\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{u}
+\left(P+\frac{B^2}{2}\right)\boldsymbol{I}
-\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}\right] &= \boldsymbol{S}_{P,g},
\label{eq:mhd-momentum}\\
\frac{\partial E_g}{\partial t}
 + \boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\left[
\left(E_g+P+\frac{B^2}{2}\right)\boldsymbol{u}
-\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{B})\right] &= S_{E,g},
\label{eq:mhd-energy}\\
\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{B}}{\partial t}
-\boldsymbol{\nabla}\times(\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{B}) &= 0,
\qquad \boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\boldsymbol{B}=0,
\label{eq:mhd-induction}\\
E_g &= \frac{P}{\gamma_g-1}
+\frac{\rho u^2}{2}+\frac{B^2}{2}.
\label{eq:mhd-total-energy}
\end{align}
The ideal-gas path uses $P=(\gamma_g-1)e_{\rm int}$. Exact-isothermal
compositions omit Equation~\ref{eq:mhd-energy} and close with
$P=c_s^2\rho$. In the full-$f$ opposite-impulse path,
$\boldsymbol{S}_{P,g}$ and $S_{E,g}$ are the negatives of the deposited
particle momentum and kinetic-energy rates defined below.

For a full-$f$ particle $p$ of species $s$, macro-particle weight $W_p$, and
macro mass
\begin{equation}
M_p=q_{\rm scale}W_p m_s,
\label{eq:macro-mass}
\end{equation}
the full-step midpoint Boris kick records the extensive rates
\begin{align}
\dot{\boldsymbol{P}}_p &=
\frac{M_p}{\Delta t}
\left(\boldsymbol{w}^{n+1}_p-\boldsymbol{w}^{n}_p\right),
\label{eq:particle-momentum-rate}\\
\dot{E}_p &=
\frac{M_p}{\Delta t}
\left[\epsilon_{\rm kin}(\boldsymbol{w}^{n+1}_p)
      -\epsilon_{\rm kin}(\boldsymbol{w}^{n}_p)\right].
\label{eq:particle-energy-rate}
\end{align}
Let ${\cal D}_i$ denote deposition to cell $i$, including the selected shape
weights and division by recipient-cell volume. At the impulse-feedback stage,
the gas update applies the opposite deposited change,
\begin{align}
(\rho\boldsymbol{u})_i &\leftarrow
(\rho\boldsymbol{u})_i-\beta\Delta t\,
{\cal D}_i(\dot{\boldsymbol{P}}_p),
\label{eq:gas-opposite-momentum}\\
E_i &\leftarrow E_i-\beta\Delta t\,{\cal D}_i(\dot{E}_p),
\label{eq:gas-opposite-energy}
\end{align}
where $\beta\Delta t$ is the active MHD stage interval. The VL2 predictor uses
the initial-position deposited charge and current to form the corresponding
Lorentz-force predictor; the corrector consumes the deposited particle
impulse. Unit momentum and energy feedback coefficients are mandatory for
\runtime. Exact-isothermal paper delta-$f$, and the separately bounded Q-006
full-$f$ carrier, use momentum-only feedback because no ideal-MHD total-energy
variable is present.

On a uniform periodic mesh, with full-$f$ particles, unit feedback
coefficients, normalized shape sums, no floors, and no particle creation,
removal, or escape, summing Equations~\ref{eq:gas-opposite-momentum} and
\ref{eq:gas-opposite-energy} over cells cancels the corresponding summed
particle changes. This is the exact global conservation invariant tested by
the uniform-mesh coupling path. It is not a generic conservation claim for
open or reflecting boundaries, shock injection and removal, delta-$f$
feedback, exact-isothermal energy, floors, or refinement interfaces; those
terms require separate accounting and qualification.

\section{Numerical Integration and Software Architecture}
\label{sec:numerics}

\subsection{Second-Order Coupled Stage Sequence}

The intended coupled sequence is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Deposit initial-position particle moments required by the gas predictor.
\item Advance particle positions to the midpoint.
\item Advance the MHD predictor and apply documented particle source terms.
\item Interpolate midpoint gas velocity and magnetic field to particles.
\item Form Equation~\ref{eq:ideal-electric-field} and advance momentum with the
relativistic Boris update.
\item Record particle momentum and kinetic-energy changes.
\item Advance particle positions to the final state.
\item Deposit recorded changes and apply the opposite gas update.
\item Apply physical boundaries, migration, synchronization, and constrained
transport in the documented task order.
\end{enumerate}
The implementation realizes this sequence with two VL2 stages having
$\beta_1=1/2$ and $\beta_2=1$. During stage 1, the particle momentum push is a
no-op: charge and current are deposited at $\boldsymbol{x}^n$, synchronized,
and used by the gas predictor through the normalized
$\rho_{\rm CR}(c\boldsymbol{E})+(\boldsymbol{J}_{\rm CR}/c)\times\boldsymbol{B}$
and $(\boldsymbol{J}_{\rm CR}/c)\cdot(c\boldsymbol{E})$ sources. Particles then
drift by $\Delta t/2$ to $\boldsymbol{x}^{n+1/2}$. During stage 2, TSC
interpolation samples the midpoint gas state, the Boris update advances
$\boldsymbol{w}$ over the full $\Delta t$, and the code deposits
$\dot{\boldsymbol{P}}_p$ and $\dot{E}_p$. The gas corrector subtracts those
deposited rates with $\beta_2=1$, after which particles drift by the remaining
$\Delta t/2$ to $\boldsymbol{x}^{n+1}$. Moment boundary exchange and
restriction/prolongation precede each gas source evaluation; particle boundary
handling and migration follow each completed stage.
