\section{Model and steady state}

For one selected passive scalar $\theta$, AthenaK evolves
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial(\rho\theta)}{\partial t}
  + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\theta\boldsymbol{u})
  = \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\kappa\nabla\theta)+\rho f ,
  \label{eq:scalar}
\end{equation}
with the velocity field $\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x})$ held fixed.  The new
driver supplies the stochastic source $f(\boldsymbol{x},t)$.  On a periodic
domain, the mass-weighted mean
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\theta}=\avg{\theta}
  \equiv \frac{1}{M}\int\rho\theta\,dV,\qquad
  M=\int\rho\,dV ,
\end{equation}
is preserved by enforcing $\avg{f}=0$ every time the forcing is rendered.
This removes the discrete zero mode even for nonuniform density or AMR.

Mean preservation is necessary but is not the mechanism that produces a
steady state.  The scalar variance
\begin{equation}
  Z=\frac{1}{2}\avg{(\theta-\bar{\theta})^2}
\end{equation}
obeys
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dZ}{dt}
  = \underbrace{\avg{(\theta-\bar{\theta})f}}_{\mathcal I}
  - \underbrace{\kappa\avg{|\nabla\theta|^2}}_{\mathcal D}.
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Thus a statistically stationary state requires $\kappa>0$ and
$\langle\mathcal I\rangle_t=\langle\mathcal D\rangle_t$.  Advection transfers
variance to small scales, where diffusion removes it.  With $\kappa=0$, a
positive variance-injection rate cannot reach a stationary variance.

\section{OU forcing}

The source is represented by nonzero Fourier modes in a configured shell,
\begin{equation}
  f_0(\boldsymbol{x},t)
  = \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}
    \operatorname{Re}\!\left[A_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t)
    e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\right],
  \qquad n_{\rm low}\leq |\boldsymbol{n}|\leq n_{\rm high}.
\end{equation}
At cadence $\Delta t_f$, each complex coefficient follows the exact
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck recurrence
\begin{equation}
 A_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{j+1}
 = r A_{\boldsymbol{k}}^j+\sqrt{1-r^2}\,\sigma_k\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^j,
 \qquad r=\exp(-\Delta t_f/\tau_f),
\end{equation}
where $\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^j$ is independent complex Gaussian noise and
$\sigma_k$ is selected by a parabolic or power-law spectrum.  The rendered
field is projected and scaled as
\begin{equation}
  f=s\left(f_0-\avg{f_0}\right).
\end{equation}
The coefficient arrays, random-number state, update count, and configuration
are restart state; the cell-centered field is regenerated from them.

Two mutually exclusive normalizations are provided:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{source\_rms}: choose $s$ so that
    $\avg{f^2}^{1/2}$ equals a specified value.
  \item \texttt{variance\_rate}: choose $s\geq0$ to inject a specified
    $\dot Z_f$ over one full step $\Delta t$.  If
    $C=\avg{(\theta-\bar{\theta})f_0}$ and $Q=\avg{f_0^2}$, solve
    \begin{equation}
      \frac{1}{2}\Delta t\,Q\,s^2+C\,s=\dot Z_f .
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The source is updated once per forcing cadence and applied during every
Runge--Kutta stage, following the turbulence-driver task ordering.  A
\texttt{scalar\_index} selects the forced scalar.  Because stochastic forcing
does not preserve pointwise bounds, signed-scalar calculations must choose an
appropriate scalar floor.

\section{History diagnostics}

The existing \texttt{gradth2\_sN} columns remain.  When scalar driving is
enabled, the user history function adds the following additive integrals for
the selected scalar; AthenaK performs the MPI sum:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Label & Integral \\
\midrule
\texttt{rho}     & $\int\rho\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rth\_sN} & $\int\rho\theta\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rth2\_sN}& $\int\rho\theta^2\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rf\_sN}  & $\int\rho f\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rthf\_sN}& $\int\rho\theta f\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rf2\_sN} & $\int\rho f^2\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rg2\_sN} & $\int\rho|\nabla\theta|^2\,dV$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These give
\begin{align}
 \bar{\theta} &= \texttt{rth}/\texttt{rho},\\
 2Z &= \texttt{rth2}/\texttt{rho}-\bar{\theta}^2,\\
 \avg{f} &= \texttt{rf}/\texttt{rho},\\
 \mathcal I &= \texttt{rthf}/\texttt{rho}
                -\bar{\theta}\,\texttt{rf}/\texttt{rho},\\
 f_{\rm rms} &= \sqrt{\texttt{rf2}/\texttt{rho}},\qquad
 \mathcal D = \kappa\,\texttt{rg2}/\texttt{rho}.
\end{align}
The mean-source column verifies conservation directly; the injection and
dissipation columns determine whether the scalar has reached statistical
steady state.
