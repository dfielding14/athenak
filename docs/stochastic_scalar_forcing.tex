\section{Model and steady state}

The intended autonomous scalar problem has a spatially uniform, frozen density
$\rho=\rho_0$ and an incompressible frozen velocity.  AthenaK stores the
conserved scalar $q=\rho\theta$ and implements
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial q}{\partial t}
  + \nabla\!\cdot(q\boldsymbol{u})
  = \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\kappa\nabla\theta)+\rho f .
  \label{eq:conservative}
\end{equation}
For $\rho=\rho_0$, division by $\rho_0$ gives the desired primitive equation
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\theta+\boldsymbol{u}\!\cdot\nabla\theta
  =\chi\nabla^2\theta+f,\qquad \chi\equiv\kappa_{\rm code}.
  \label{eq:scalar}
\end{equation}
This equivalence is not true for a spatially varying density.  In that case
the implemented primitive diffusion operator is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\kappa\nabla\theta)
 =\kappa\nabla^2\theta+\kappa\nabla\ln\rho\!\cdot\nabla\theta
 \quad\hbox{for constant }\kappa .
\end{equation}
Thus the standard constant-$\chi$ problem below assumes uniform frozen
density; choosing $\kappa\propto1/\rho$ does not recover
$\chi\nabla^2\theta$ after division by $\rho$.

The new driver supplies $f(\boldsymbol{x},t)$.  On a periodic domain, the
mass-weighted mean
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\theta}=\avg{\theta}
  \equiv \frac{1}{M}\int\rho\theta\,dV,\qquad
  M=\int\rho\,dV ,
\end{equation}
is preserved by enforcing $\avg{f}=0$ every time the forcing is rendered.
This removes the discrete zero mode even for nonuniform density or AMR.

Mean preservation is necessary but is not the mechanism that produces a
steady state.  The scalar variance
\begin{equation}
  Z=\frac{1}{2}\avg{(\theta-\bar{\theta})^2}
\end{equation}
obeys
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dZ}{dt}
  = \underbrace{\avg{(\theta-\bar{\theta})f}}_{\mathcal I}
  - \underbrace{\chi\avg{|\nabla\theta|^2}}_{\varepsilon_\theta}.
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Thus a statistically stationary state requires $\chi>0$ and
$\langle\mathcal I\rangle_t=\langle\varepsilon_\theta\rangle_t$.  Advection transfers
variance to small scales, where diffusion removes it.  The controlled
finite-resolution experiment therefore uses $\chi>0$.  At $\chi=0$, any
stationary balance in the code is numerical unless an inviscid dissipative
limit has first been established by a resolved $\chi\to0$ sequence.

\section{OU forcing}

The source is represented by nonzero Fourier modes in a configured shell,
\begin{equation}
  f_0(\boldsymbol{x},t)
  = \sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}
    \operatorname{Re}\!\left[A_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t)
    e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\right],
  \qquad n_{\rm low}\leq |\boldsymbol{n}|\leq n_{\rm high}.
\end{equation}
At cadence $\Delta t_f$, each complex coefficient follows the exact
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck recurrence
\begin{equation}
 A_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{j+1}
 = r A_{\boldsymbol{k}}^j+\sqrt{1-r^2}\,\sigma_k\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^j,
 \qquad r=\exp(-\Delta t_f/\tau_f),
\end{equation}
where $\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}^j$ is independent complex Gaussian noise and
$\sigma_k$ is selected by a parabolic or power-law spectrum.  The rendered
field is projected and scaled as
\begin{equation}
  f=s\left(f_0-\avg{f_0}\right).
\end{equation}
The coefficient arrays, random-number state, update count, and configuration
are restart state; the cell-centered field is regenerated from them.

Two mutually exclusive normalizations are provided:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{source\_rms}: choose $s$ so that
    $\avg{f^2}^{1/2}$ equals a specified value.
  \item \texttt{variance\_rate}: choose $s\geq0$ to inject a specified
    $\dot Z_f$ over one full step $\Delta t$.  If
    $C=\avg{(\theta-\bar{\theta})f_0}$ and $Q=\avg{f_0^2}$, solve
    \begin{equation}
      \frac{1}{2}\Delta t\,Q\,s^2+C\,s=\dot Z_f .
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The modal OU state is updated at the forcing cadence; the field is rendered,
projected, and normalized once per cycle and applied during every
Runge--Kutta stage, following the turbulence-driver task ordering.  A
\texttt{scalar\_index} selects the forced scalar.  Because stochastic forcing
does not preserve pointwise bounds, signed-scalar calculations must choose an
appropriate scalar floor.

\section{History diagnostics}

The existing \texttt{gradth2\_sN} columns remain.  When scalar driving is
enabled, the user history function adds the following additive integrals for
the selected scalar; AthenaK performs the MPI sum:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Label & Integral \\
\midrule
\texttt{rho}     & $\int\rho\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rth\_sN} & $\int\rho\theta\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rth2\_sN}& $\int\rho\theta^2\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rf\_sN}  & $\int\rho f\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rthf\_sN}& $\int\rho\theta f\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rf2\_sN} & $\int\rho f^2\,dV$ \\
\texttt{rg2\_sN} & $\int\rho|\nabla\theta|^2\,dV$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These give
\begin{align}
 \bar{\theta} &= \texttt{rth}/\texttt{rho},\\
 2Z &= \texttt{rth2}/\texttt{rho}-\bar{\theta}^2,\\
 \avg{f} &= \texttt{rf}/\texttt{rho},\\
 \mathcal I &= \texttt{rthf}/\texttt{rho}
                -\bar{\theta}\,\texttt{rf}/\texttt{rho},\\
 f_{\rm rms} &= \sqrt{\texttt{rf2}/\texttt{rho}},\qquad
 \varepsilon_\theta = \chi\,\texttt{rg2}/\texttt{rho}.
\end{align}
The mean-source column verifies conservation directly; the injection and
dissipation columns determine whether the scalar has reached statistical
steady state.  Binary output with
\texttt{variable = scalar\_force} stores the cell-centered $f$ required for
the finite-separation forcing term in the Yaglom budget below.

\section{Anomalous dissipation and Yaglom's law}

\paragraph{Vanishing-diffusivity protocol.}
For each autonomous velocity realization, keep the solver-effective velocity,
OU seed, forcing shell, correlation time, and forcing normalization fixed
while taking a jointly resolved sequence $\chi\to0$.  The cleanest anomaly
test uses \texttt{source\_rms}, because the forcing law is then independent of
both $\theta$ and $\chi$.  After a burn-in interval, require simultaneously
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\texttt{rf}}{\texttt{rho}}\simeq0,\qquad
 \frac{d\bar{\theta}}{dt}\simeq0,\qquad
 \left\langle\frac{dZ}{dt}\right\rangle_t\simeq0,\qquad
 \langle\mathcal I\rangle_t\simeq
 \langle\varepsilon_\theta\rangle_t .
\end{equation}
The residual
$\mathcal I-dZ/dt-\varepsilon_\theta$ estimates numerical dissipation and
must decrease under grid refinement before
$\varepsilon_\theta=\chi\langle|\nabla\theta|^2\rangle$ is interpreted as a
physical dissipation rate.
At every $\chi$, repeat at higher resolution until
$\langle\varepsilon_\theta\rangle_t$ and the scalar structure functions are
grid-converged and the diffusive cutoff is resolved.  Evidence for a
statistically stationary dissipation anomaly is then
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{\chi\to0}\langle\varepsilon_\theta\rangle_t>0
 \quad\hbox{together with bounded, converged }Z
 \hbox{ and large-scale statistics}.
\end{equation}
A positive dissipation alone is not sufficient when
\texttt{variance\_rate} is used, because that option deliberately fixes the
source-only variance input.  In that case the nontrivial evidence is
convergence of the stationary scalar measure, a resolved constant flux, and
the Yaglom balance.  This permanent-regime test complements, rather than
replaces, the unforced finite-time diagnostic
$\chi\int_0^T\langle|\nabla\theta|^2\rangle\,dt$.

\paragraph{Scale-by-scale test.}
Define increments $\delta_{\boldsymbol r}\theta
=\theta(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol r)-\theta(\boldsymbol{x})$ and similarly
$\delta_{\boldsymbol r}\boldsymbol u$ and $\delta_{\boldsymbol r}f$.  For
uniform density, homogeneous statistics, and incompressible advection,
\begin{equation}
 \partial_t S_2+
 \nabla_{\boldsymbol r}\!\cdot
 \left\langle\delta_{\boldsymbol r}\boldsymbol u
                  (\delta_{\boldsymbol r}\theta)^2\right\rangle
 =2\chi\nabla_{\boldsymbol r}^2S_2-4\varepsilon_\theta
  +2\left\langle\delta_{\boldsymbol r}\theta\,
                    \delta_{\boldsymbol r}f\right\rangle ,
 \label{eq:yaglom-full}
\end{equation}
where $S_2=\langle(\delta_{\boldsymbol r}\theta)^2\rangle$.  If the OU forcing
is confined to large scales, then for
$\ell_\chi\ll r\ll\ell_f$ the last and diffusive terms are negligible.  The
isotropic longitudinal mixed structure function must then satisfy
\begin{equation}
 Y(r)\equiv
 \left\langle\delta_ru_L(\delta_r\theta)^2\right\rangle
 =-\frac{4}{d}\,\varepsilon_\theta r .
 \label{eq:yaglom}
\end{equation}
The coefficient is $-2$ in two dimensions and $-4/3$ in three dimensions.
The primary test is the compensated plateau
$-dY/(4\varepsilon_\theta r)\simeq1$ over the same range in which the full
budget \eqref{eq:yaglom-full} closes.  If
$\delta_ru\sim r^\alpha$ and
$S_2(r)\sim r^{2\beta}$ with a single scaling exponent, the linear mixed
flux implies $\alpha+2\beta=1$; the exact mixed third-order law is stronger
than fitting those two exponents separately.
For one frozen velocity realization, compute the isotropic sector by
averaging over positions and separation directions, and repeat over forcing
seeds or velocity realizations to quantify residual finite-box anisotropy.

\section{Comparison with mean-gradient forcing}

Writing the total scalar as
$T(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\boldsymbol G\!\cdot\boldsymbol{x}+\theta(\boldsymbol{x},t)$
gives the periodic fluctuation equation
\begin{equation}
 \partial_t\theta+\boldsymbol u\!\cdot\nabla\theta
 =\chi\nabla^2\theta-\boldsymbol G\!\cdot\boldsymbol u .
 \label{eq:mean-gradient}
\end{equation}
Its mean is preserved when $\langle\boldsymbol u\rangle=0$, and its variance
input is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_G=-\boldsymbol G\!\cdot\langle\boldsymbol u\theta\rangle .
\end{equation}
The existing AthenaK source adds
\texttt{+mean\_gradient}$\,v_x$; this corresponds to
$\boldsymbol G=-\texttt{mean\_gradient}\,\hat{\boldsymbol x}$ in
\eqref{eq:mean-gradient}.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.20\linewidth}p{0.35\linewidth}p{0.35\linewidth}}
\toprule
 & OU scalar forcing & Mean-gradient forcing \\
\midrule
Injection &
Independent stochastic field in a chosen low-$k$ shell. &
$-\boldsymbol G\!\cdot\boldsymbol u$; tied to the velocity spectrum. \\
Symmetry &
Homogeneous and isotropic in the ensemble when the shell sampling is isotropic. &
Selects $\boldsymbol G$ and is anisotropic. \\
Primary use &
Clean stationary cascade, dissipation anomaly, and Yaglom flux. &
Turbulent scalar flux and effective diffusivity
$\langle u_i\theta\rangle=-K_{ij}G_j$. \\
Autonomous velocity &
Produces a time-stationary scalar process through the OU coefficients. &
The source is time independent and may relax to one steady spatial solution. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For the periodic fluctuation $\theta$, mean-gradient forcing contributes
\begin{equation}
 2\langle\delta_r\theta\,\delta_r f_G\rangle
 =-2\boldsymbol G\!\cdot
   \langle\delta_r\boldsymbol u\,\delta_r\theta\rangle
\end{equation}
to \eqref{eq:yaglom-full}.  Therefore the simple compensated Yaglom law may
not hold unless this production term is negligible, or unless one projects
the statistics of the total field $T$ onto the isotropic sector.  This is
especially important here because a broadband rough autonomous velocity
makes $-\boldsymbol G\!\cdot\boldsymbol u$ a broadband source.  The OU option
provides cleaner separation between forcing, inertial, and diffusive scales;
the mean-gradient option provides the cleaner measurement of transport
relative to an imposed macroscopic gradient.  Results from the two protocols
should be compared as distinct forcing ensembles, not pooled without checking
their full scale-by-scale budgets.

\section{Frontier execution}

The Frontier launcher uses eight MPI ranks per node and binds one
\texttt{gfx90a} GCD to each rank.  The forcing normalization evaluates the
five raw moments needed for mean projection and normalization in one Kokkos
reduction followed by one \texttt{MPI\_Allreduce} per forcing refresh; the
projected, normalized source is then formed in one device kernel.  This is
algebraically identical to projecting first and measuring the projected
moments in a second pass, but removes one device reduction, one global
collective, and one device kernel from every refresh.

The production input writes binary fields and restart payloads with
\texttt{single\_file\_per\_node=true}.  Submit the supplied batch script as
\begin{verbatim}
sbatch -A PROJECT_ID -N NODES scripts/run_frontier_scalar_ou.sbatch
\end{verbatim}
and override input parameters after the script name when needed.  The build
and run scripts default to an OLCF-listed GPU-aware CPE/CCE/ROCm/Cray-MPICH
tuple; module overrides must be made as a compatible set.
